Referral Programmes for Music Schools: Fair Rewards, Clear Rules

News · 2026-11-11

A referral programme is a simple system in which current students or families who recommend a music school receive a small thank-you when the person they recommend enrols. For many studios, personal recommendations already bring in a large share of new learners, so a referral programme does not create word of mouth from nothing. Instead, it makes existing recommendations easier to share and shows appreciation in a consistent, transparent way.

Start with an experience worth recommending

No reward can fix lessons that people do not enjoy. Before designing a programme, look honestly at the basics: whether lessons start on time, whether families understand progress, and whether questions get answered quickly. If those are in good shape, a referral programme can amplify goodwill that already exists.

It also helps to ask current families why they chose the school. Their answers reveal what referrers will actually say to friends, which shapes the wording of your invitation. You may discover that families value something you rarely mention, such as flexible rescheduling or friendly reception staff, and that detail can become the heart of the referral message.

Choosing a fair reward

The reward should feel generous enough to be noticed but not so large that people recommend the school only for the gift.

  • Lesson credit, such as one free lesson for the referrer once the new student completes a first month.
  • A two-sided reward, giving both the referrer and the new student a small benefit.
  • Non-financial thanks, such as a printed certificate for children or early access to recital registration.
  • A donation option, where the school gives a small amount to a local music project instead.

Whatever you choose, make it simple to explain in one sentence.

Writing clear rules

Clear rules prevent disappointment and disputes later. Write them in plain language and publish them where families can find them.

Define a successful referral

Is it a trial lesson, a paid month or a full term? Choose a milestone that shows the new student genuinely joined.

Set limits and timing

Explain when the reward is given, whether rewards can be combined, and whether there is a cap per family.

Plan for exceptions

Decide in advance what happens if two people refer the same person or if a new student leaves quickly.

Respecting privacy and consent

Referral programmes involve personal data, so take care. Do not ask current students to hand over friends' phone numbers or email addresses. Instead, give the referrer a link or a short code they can share, and let the new person contact the school themselves. This keeps the school in line with Thai privacy legislation (the PDPA) and avoids unwanted contact. It also means the new family arrives because they chose to, which tends to make the first conversation warmer.

When you thank a referrer, avoid revealing details about the new student without permission.

Spreading the word without pressure

Announce the programme once in a newsletter, mention it on the website and remind families occasionally, for example at the start of a new term. Avoid constant reminders or messages implying that the offer is about to disappear. A calm tone keeps the programme feeling like appreciation rather than a sales drive.

Digital tools can make sharing easier. A school whose students also use an app such as TIGA ALPHA between lessons might let learners share a short practice clip, with their consent, alongside a referral link, so friends see real learning rather than an advertisement.

Reviewing the numbers each term

Track how many new students mention a referral, how long referred students stay and how many referrers participate. Review these figures every term, and add a short note on what changed, such as a new timetable or a new teacher, since outside factors can move referral numbers as much as the programme itself. If referrals are steady but rewards are rarely claimed, the process may be too complicated. If many people sign up but leave quickly, the milestone may be too early.

FAQ

Q: Should children be allowed to refer friends?

A: The parent or guardian should take part, so rewards and communication go through an adult.

Q: Is cash a good referral reward?

A: Lesson credit or other non-cash rewards usually fit a school better and keep the focus on learning.

Q: How do we track referrals fairly?

A: Use a personal code or link for each referrer and ask new students on the sign-up form who recommended them.

Q: Can referral programmes feel pushy?

A: They can if promoted too often. Announce clearly, remind occasionally and let satisfied families decide.
