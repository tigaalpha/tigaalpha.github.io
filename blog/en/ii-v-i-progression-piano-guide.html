What to remember

  • iiâ€“Vâ€“I describes chord functions, so it works in any key.
  • The sound comes from tension (the V chord) resolving to rest (the I chord).
  • Two notes per chord, the 3rd and the 7th, carry most of the harmony.

Why does it sound so satisfying?

The secret is smooth voice movement: the way individual notes travel from one chord to the next.

  • The 7th of Dm7 (C) slides down a half step to B, the 3rd of G7.
  • The 7th of G7 (F) slides down a half step to E, the 3rd of Cmaj7.

Those tiny half-step moves create a strong sense of motion. G7 also contains a tritone (B and F), an unstable interval that "wants" to resolve. When it does, your ear hears arrival.

Once the left hand feels steady, add one right-hand note per chord, for example A over Dm7, B over G7 and C over Cmaj7. You are now starting to play music rather than just an exercise.

Common mistakes and quick fixes

  • Rushing the change to V. Count every beat out loud.
  • Heavy, muddy chords. Use shells and keep the left hand in the middle of the keyboard.
  • Only practicing in C. Even two extra keys a week make a noticeable difference over time.
  • Ignoring the ear. Sing the guide tones (3rds and 7ths) softly while you play.
