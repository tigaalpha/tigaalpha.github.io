Camera Angles for Online Piano Lessons: Showing Your Hands Clearly

Practice tips · 2026-11-16

Online lessons make piano learning flexible, but a teacher can only help with what they can see. A camera that shows the hands, the keys and the learner's posture makes the lesson more useful. The good news is that no special equipment is needed. A phone or laptop and a few small adjustments are enough. This article offers practical set-up ideas for learners and parents.

What the teacher needs to see

The most useful view shows both hands on the keys, with the notes they are playing. A second view, from a little further back, shows the posture and the position of the arms. Teachers often ask for both views in the first lesson, then choose the one that works best.

  • Hands and keys from above or at a slight angle.
  • Face and upper body from the front or side.
  • The music stand, if the teacher wants to check the score.

Option one: one camera from the side

If there is only one device, place it to the side of the piano, a little in front of the learner, pointing across the keyboard. This angle shows the hands, wrists, arms and posture together. It is a good all-round choice for beginners.

Check the distance

Move the camera far enough back to see both elbows, but close enough to see the fingers.

Check the height

Place the device at about the height of the keys or slightly above, so the angle is not too steep.

Option two: two devices

If a second device is available, use one for the hands and one for the face and body. A phone on a small stand above the keys can show the hands, while the laptop shows the learner. Make sure both are stable. Mute the microphone on one device to avoid echo and feedback.

Lighting matters

Dim light makes the keys hard to see. Face the piano towards a window or add a soft lamp. Avoid a bright light behind the learner, which turns hands into dark shapes. Even light on the keys and the face is ideal.

  • Switch on a lamp near the piano.
  • Avoid shiny reflections on the keyboard.
  • Turn off flashing or very bright lights.

Sound and picture together

Piano sound can overload a small microphone, making it harsh. Try placing the device further from the piano or lowering the volume of the instrument. If the sound is still unclear, the teacher can also listen on a recording sent after the lesson. Ask the teacher what works best.

Stability and safety

Keep devices on a steady stand or a stack of books, away from the edge of the piano or table. Watch cables so that no one trips. For children, an adult should be nearby and should help set up the camera and check the privacy of the room.

Privacy and kindness

Share only what the lesson needs. Check what appears in the background of the camera and tidy the room if needed. Parents should be present for young children, and families should only use trusted video tools. Do not record the lesson unless everyone involved agrees.

Practice between lessons

Between lessons, learners can keep the camera set-up and film a short clip of their practice, if the family agrees, to show the teacher. A practice app can also help. Teacher and learner agreements

At the start of the term, agree on a few simple habits. The learner joins a couple of minutes early to test the picture and sound. The teacher says when the view needs adjusting, and the learner moves the camera calmly without rushing. If the connection drops, both sides agree on what to do next, such as calling back or continuing by message. These small agreements save lesson time and keep everyone relaxed, especially younger children who may feel worried when the screen freezes.

A quick checklist before each lesson

  • Is the device charged and steady?
  • Can the teacher see both hands?
  • Is the light bright enough?
  • Is the sound clear?
  • Is the room quiet and tidy?

FAQ

Q: Do I need an expensive camera?

A: No. A phone or laptop camera is usually enough.

Q: Which angle is best for children?

A: A side view that shows hands and posture works well. The teacher can ask for changes.

Q: What if my internet is slow?

A: Close other apps, move closer to the router and ask the teacher about lower picture quality.

Q: Can I record the lesson?

A: Only if everyone agrees. Always respect privacy.
