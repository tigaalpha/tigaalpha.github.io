The Sostenuto Pedal: Holding Some Notes While Others Stay Clear

Practice tips · 2026-11-11

Most pianists know the right pedal, which sustains every note, and the left pedal, which softens the sound. The middle pedal is often a mystery. On many grand pianos and some uprights and digital pianos, it is a sostenuto pedal: a pedal that sustains only the notes being held at the moment it is pressed. Everything played afterwards remains unsustained unless the right pedal is also used. This selective sustain opens up textures that would otherwise be impossible with two hands.

How the sostenuto works

When you press a key, its damper lifts off the string. If you then press the sostenuto pedal while the key is still down, a mechanism catches that damper and keeps it raised. You can release the key and the note will continue to ring. Any keys you play later have their dampers working normally, so they stop sounding when released.

The order is essential:

  • play and hold the notes you want to sustain
  • press the sostenuto pedal
  • release the keys; the held notes keep sounding
  • play other notes freely, with clear articulation
  • release the pedal to stop the sustained notes

If you press the pedal before playing the notes, nothing is caught.

Checking your instrument

Not every middle pedal is a sostenuto. On many upright pianos, the middle pedal is a practice mute that lowers a strip of felt between hammers and strings. On some instruments, it sustains only the bass. Test yours by holding a chord, pressing the middle pedal, releasing the keys and playing staccato notes elsewhere. If the chord continues and the staccato notes stay short, you have a true sostenuto. Many digital pianos include a sostenuto function that can be assigned to a pedal.

When composers ask for it

The sostenuto pedal became more widely used in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Composers write "sost. ped." or a separate line below the staff to indicate it. Typical uses include:

  • holding a deep bass note while the hands play clear, detached chords above it
  • sustaining a chord in the middle register while a melody moves with changing harmonies
  • creating resonance by silently pressing keys, catching them with the sostenuto, and letting other notes make the undamped strings vibrate

Even in older music where it is not marked, some pianists use the sostenuto discreetly to keep a bass note ringing through harmony changes that would blur with the right pedal.

Combining pedals

The sostenuto can be used together with the right pedal. A common technique is to catch a bass note with the sostenuto, then use the right pedal for the upper harmonies, changing it as needed. When the right pedal is released, the bass note continues because the sostenuto still holds it. This takes coordination, since the left foot operates the middle pedal while the right foot handles the sustain.

Practising the timing

Start with one note

Play a low C, hold it, press the sostenuto, release the key and play short chords in the right hand. Listen for the bass continuing alone.

Add a chord

Repeat with a full chord in the left hand, then play a melody in the right hand without the right pedal.

Coordinate with the right pedal

Once the sostenuto feels natural, add the right pedal for the upper part and practise changing it while the bass stays.

Watch the timing of the catch

If the pedal is pressed too late, after the keys are released, the dampers fall and the notes stop. Practise slowly so the catch happens while the keys are firmly down.

Musical judgement

Like any pedal, the sostenuto is a tool, not a rule. Use it to clarify textures, not to sustain everything. Listen for whether the held notes support the music or start to clash with later harmonies. Releasing at the right moment matters as much as catching the notes.

FAQ

Q: Is the middle pedal always a sostenuto?

A: No. On many uprights it is a practice mute, and on some instruments it sustains only the bass.

Q: Why does nothing happen when I press the sostenuto?

A: The notes must already be held down when you press the pedal; otherwise no dampers are caught.

Q: Can I use the sostenuto and the right pedal together?

A: Yes. This allows a bass note to ring while the upper harmonies are pedalled and changed separately.

Q: Which foot operates the sostenuto?

A: Usually the left foot, leaving the right foot free for the sustain pedal.
