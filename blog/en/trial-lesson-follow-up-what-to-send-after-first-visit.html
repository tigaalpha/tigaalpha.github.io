Trial Lesson Follow-Up: What to Send After the First Visit

News · 2026-11-15

A trial lesson is often the first time a family meets a teacher. What the studio sends afterwards can shape the whole relationship. A good follow-up is brief, warm and useful, and it leaves the decision to the family. This guide describes what to include, using general ideas and an example scenario instead of real data.

Send a message that is easy to read

A short email or chat message works best. People are busy, and a message that can be read in one minute is more likely to be read at all.

  • Thank the family for visiting.
  • Mention one specific thing that went well, such as steady hands or curious questions.
  • Offer one or two simple next steps.
  • Say how to ask questions.

Be specific but kind

Describe what the student did, not what the student is. "Today we found middle C and played a short pattern" is better than a broad judgement.

Include a small home idea

A simple activity helps the learner feel that the trial continues at home. It might be finding the same note on any keyboard, clapping a rhythm or listening to a favourite song and tapping the beat. Keep it optional and short.

Explain what happens next

Families should know the options without being pushed. Describe the regular lesson time, what a first month looks like and how to enrol, in plain language. If there is a waiting list or limited timetable, say so honestly.

  • List the next steps in the order a family would take them.
  • State fees once, clearly, and avoid countdowns.
  • Say that it is fine to take time to decide.

What to leave out

  • Promises about progress or exam results.
  • Comparisons with other families or other studios.
  • Pressure words such as "last chance".
  • Long attachments that nobody opens.

Picturing a good follow-up

Imagine a small studio that sends a three-paragraph note the same evening. The first paragraph thanks the family, the second describes what the child enjoyed and the third lists two options for the next step. The studio also attaches a one-page sheet about practice at home. Parents tell the teacher that the note made them feel welcome and unhurried. The scenario is made up to show the idea and is not about a real studio.

Helping practice in the first weeks

Many new learners benefit from a gentle routine in the first weeks. A studio may suggest a few minutes a day with a keyboard. Some families also try TIGA ALPHA, an AI piano teaching app that follows notes and timing and suggests which bar to repeat. Mention such tools as options, and keep the teacher at the centre.

Following up once, not repeatedly

If the family does not reply, one polite reminder after a reasonable time is enough. After that, leave the door open and let them return when they are ready.

Adjusting the message for different learners

A message to the parent of a young child will differ from one to an adult learner. For a child, mention what the child enjoyed and how a parent can help at home without taking over. For an adult, mention the pace and the kinds of pieces that could come next. In both cases, use a friendly voice and avoid technical words unless they are explained.

Keeping notes for the teacher

After the trial, the teacher can jot down a few observations: what the learner already knew, which hand felt easier and which pieces sparked interest. These notes help plan the first lessons and make the follow-up message feel personal. Store them privately and share only what is helpful.

A closing line that feels human

End the message with a simple sentence that invites questions, such as "If anything is unclear, just reply and we will be glad to help." Add the teacher's first name so that the reader knows a real person wrote it. Avoid a long signature full of links, because a clean ending is easier to trust.

FAQ

Q: How soon should the follow-up go out?

A: The same day or the next day is usually good, while the visit is fresh.

Q: Should the message include prices?

A: Yes, stated once and clearly, so families do not need to ask.

Q: What if the family decides not to continue?

A: Thank them, wish the learner well and leave the door open.

Q: Is it fine to include a home activity?

A: Yes, as a short optional idea.
