Photography at Studio Recitals: Clear Rules for Families

News · 2026-11-12

A recital photography policy is a short, written rule that tells families and guests what they may photograph or film at a studio concert, when devices should be put down, and how official pictures, if any, will be taken and shared. The aim is not to spoil a proud parent’s record of the day. The aim is to protect performers who do not wish to be filmed, to keep a quiet enough room, and to follow consent and privacy rules. A policy that arrives only as a whisper at the door is already too late.

The rule should respect the law that applies at the venue, and in Thailand when the concert is held there, especially where children are being photographed. What follows is a planning sketch, not a legal opinion.

What to decide, and what to say in advance

The studio should decide, and then publish, a few points before tickets or invitations go out. May family members photograph their own child only, or not at all during a piece? Are still photos allowed after a bow, but not during playing? Is video reserved for one official camera, or forbidden? Where should people stand if a photo is allowed, so that aisles and other families are not blocked? How will the official pictures, if they exist, be shared, and for how long will they be kept?

These decisions should appear in the invitation, in a reminder message, and on a short sign at the door, in the same wording. If a family cannot attend without making their own video, it is kinder to say so in advance than to argue in the hall.

A compact set of lines is often enough:

  • No photography or filming during performances, unless the invitation has said otherwise.
  • Devices silent, and screens dim, while someone is playing.
  • Official photos, if planned, will be taken at a named moment, with a stated consent process.
  • Families may opt out of official photos in writing before the day.
  • Any later public use of a picture needs its own clear permission.

Consent, children and later use

A smile in a corridor is not the same as permission to post a child’s face on a public page, or to use a performance video in an advertisement. If the studio wants official photos, it should say who will take them, where they may appear, and how a family can refuse or later ask for a picture to be taken down, within the limits of what has already been printed or legally must be kept. Guests who are not in the studio’s usual records also need a way to be told the rule, which is why the door sign matters.

During a piece, the performer has the first claim on the room

A device held in a sightline, a shutter sound, or a parent walking into the aisle can undo a student’s careful start. Even when photos are allowed after a bow, they can wait a few seconds for the last sound to die away. A gentle, pre-agreed announcement is more effective than a scolding after the third phone appears.

Teachers, accompanists and shared programmes

One photography rule for a shared hall

If two studios share a hall, or if an accompanist is visiting, agree one photography rule for the event and print only that rule. After the recital, official files should be stored carefully, and unofficial clips should not be requested from random guests as a substitute for a plan. Ordinary teaching the next week, including a short later session with TIGA ALPHA if that is already part of a student’s home work, does not need the recital video unless a family has been told, and has agreed, that a recording will be used for review.

FAQ

Q: Can a parent film only their own child?

A: Only if the policy allows it. Other students, and sometimes a formal hall rule, may still be in the frame. The written event rule is the one to follow.

Q: What if a family refuses all official photos?

A: That refusal should be respected, and the official photographer should have a list of students who must not appear.

Q: Should the teacher stop a piece to ask for phones to be put down?

A: A short announcement before a group of pieces is usually enough. Stopping mid-performance is a last resort.

Q: Can recital photos be used later in a studio brochure?

A: Only with a clear, separate permission for that use, especially when children are pictured.

Q: Does a public hall have its own rule?

A: Often yes. The stricter of the hall’s rule and the studio’s rule should be the one announced.
