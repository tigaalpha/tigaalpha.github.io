How Fans Find Concerts: Follow Buttons, Tour Alerts and Local Reach

News · 2026-11-01

Most fans now learn about concerts through alerts rather than posters. Concert-discovery services and the follow buttons built into streaming and social platforms record which artists a listener likes, then send a notification when one of those artists announces a show in the listener's city. For musicians, this means a tour date is only half of the job: the other half is making sure the right people follow you in the places you plan to play, and that your event details are listed accurately wherever fans look.

This guide explains how concert discovery works and offers practical steps for independent musicians, ensembles and teachers who organise student concerts.

The discovery chain

Following

A listener taps follow on an artist page, or a service infers interest from listening history and imported music libraries.

Location

The service uses the listener's chosen city or device location to match nearby events.

Announcement

When the artist or a promoter adds a date, the system pushes a message to matching followers.

Action

The alert links to ticket sales, an RSVP button or event details. Some services remind fans again close to the date.

Why it changed live music marketing

Before these tools, a band relied on local press, radio, flyers and word of mouth, and it was easy for a fan to miss a show entirely. Alerts reach interested people directly and cost little. They also give artists data, such as where followers cluster, which helps when planning routes for a tour or choosing a venue size.

Where musicians lose fans

Even with good tools, many small acts miss opportunities.

  • Event details appear on one site but not others, so some followers never hear about the show.
  • Dates are announced too late for fans to plan.
  • Artist pages are incomplete, with no photo, bio or recent music.
  • Fans are never actually asked to follow the artist.

A simple routine for independent artists

  • Ask at every performance and in every video for people to follow you on the platforms you use.
  • List every confirmed date as soon as possible, with venue, time and ticket link checked for accuracy.
  • Announce in waves: first to your mailing list, then publicly, then a reminder a week before.
  • Look at where followers live and consider adding cities with strong clusters.
  • After the show, thank attendees and share a short clip so that people who missed it feel encouraged to come next time.

For piano teachers and student concerts

Studio recitals can use the same thinking on a smaller scale. A simple event page, a shared calendar invitation and two reminder messages to families often improve attendance. Inviting relatives to follow the studio's channels before the event means they will hear about the next concert automatically. Keep personal details private, share only what families agree to, and avoid posting children's full names publicly.

Writing an event listing people act on

A clear listing answers every question before a fan has to ask. Put the date, start time, venue name and address in the first lines. Add a one-sentence description of the music, such as solo piano with original songs or a student showcase with duets. Mention ticket price or free entry, age suitability and accessibility information. Finish with a single, obvious link. Listings that look complete and trustworthy are shared more often, because followers feel confident inviting friends.

Online events count too

Livestreams, online masterclasses and virtual recitals can be listed like physical shows. Followers in other cities or countries who cannot travel can still attend, and the same alert and reminder habits apply. Clear start times with time zones avoid confusion for international audiences.

A note on timing

Many fans decide in the final days, so a reminder close to the event often brings a second wave of interest.

Reading the results

Track how many people clicked the event link, how many tickets or RSVPs followed and how many new followers appeared after the show. Over several events, patterns appear: which announcement timing worked, which city responded and which format, such as solo piano or a band setting, drew the most interest.

FAQ

Q: How do concert-alert services know which shows to tell me about?

A: They match the artists you follow or listen to with events announced near your chosen location.

Q: When should a musician announce a show?

A: Early enough for fans to plan, often several weeks ahead, with reminders close to the date.

Q: Do small artists benefit from tour alerts?

A: Yes. Even a small group of local followers receiving a timely alert can fill a modest venue.

Q: Can student recitals use the same methods?

A: Yes. Event pages, calendar invitations and reminders help families attend, while keeping personal data private.
