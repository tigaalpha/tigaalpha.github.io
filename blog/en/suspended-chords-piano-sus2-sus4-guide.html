Suspended Chords on Piano: Sus2 and Sus4 Made Simple

Music theory · 2026-11-04

A suspended chord replaces the third of a triad with either the second or the fourth note of the scale. Without the third, the chord is neither clearly major nor clearly minor, so it sounds open and slightly unfinished. Csus2 is C, D and G; Csus4 is C, F and G. On piano they are easy to build from a normal triad by moving one finger, and they are useful for intros, gentle tension before a resolution and modern pop accompaniment.

What "suspended" means

The word comes from older harmony. In choral music, a note from one chord was often held over into the next chord, where it clashed briefly before stepping down to a note that fit. That held note was "suspended" above the new harmony. In classical writing the suspension almost always resolves, usually from the fourth down to the third.

In modern pop, rock and worship music, the name stayed but the rule relaxed. A sus chord may resolve, or it may simply sit there as a colour in its own right. Both uses are worth knowing.

Building sus chords from a triad

Start with any major or minor triad in root position. Find the middle note, which is the third.

  • For sus2, move the third down to the second note of the scale. C–E–G becomes C–D–G.
  • For sus4, move the third up to the fourth note of the scale. C–E–G becomes C–F–G.
  • Because the third is gone, Cm and C both become the same Csus2 or Csus4.

Try it in a few keys. In G, Gsus2 is G–A–D and Gsus4 is G–C–D. In D, Dsus2 is D–E–A and Dsus4 is D–G–A. Notice that every sus chord still contains the root and the fifth, which keep it anchored.

A useful observation

Csus2 contains the same notes as Gsus4 in a different order: C, D and G. Sus chords can therefore be named in more than one way depending on the bass note. On a lead sheet, the bass note tells you which name the writer intended.

How sus chords resolve

The traditional move is sus4 to major: the fourth slides down a half step or a whole step to the third. Play Csus4 and then C, keeping the outer notes still and moving only the middle finger from F to E. You will hear a gentle sense of arrival.

Sus2 often resolves up: the second rises to the third. Play Csus2 and then C, moving the middle note from D to E.

A very common pattern in songs is the "sus4 sigh": a chord is played as sus4 for one beat, then as major for the rest of the bar. It adds movement to a long chord without changing the harmony.

Using sus chords in accompaniment

Intros. Many songs open on a sus chord because it sounds expectant. Holding Dsus4 before a verse in D creates a small sense of waiting.

Long chords. If a chord lasts two bars, try alternating sus4, major, sus2, major. The bass stays the same, but the inner voice moves and keeps the accompaniment alive.

Dominant sus. In jazz and pop, a chord such as G7sus4 or F/G is often used in place of a plain G7. It softens the pull toward C while still pointing there.

Open voicings. Spread a sus2 chord across two hands, for example C in the left hand and D–G–C in the right. The result has a wide, airy sound that suits slow ballads.

Ear-training with sus chords

Play a major chord, a minor chord and a sus chord on the same root, one after another, and listen to the difference. The major sounds bright, the minor sounds darker, and the sus sounds neutral or floating. Then ask a friend or family member to play one of the three while you guess. This small game trains you to recognise the open colour quickly when you hear it in recordings.

Common questions about notation

Lead sheets may write sus chords as Csus, Csus4, Csus2 or C2. Csus without a number usually means sus4. C2 sometimes means sus2 and sometimes means a major chord with an added second, so check the context or the written notes if they are available. Cadd9 is different again: it keeps the third and adds the ninth on top.

FAQ

Q: Are sus chords major or minor?

A: Neither. Without the third, they do not belong to either quality, which is why they sound open.

Q: Do sus chords always need to resolve?

A: In classical harmony they usually do. In modern songs they often stand alone as a colour.

Q: What is the difference between sus2 and add2?

A: Sus2 replaces the third with the second. Add2 keeps the third and also includes the second.

Q: Which fingers should I use for a sus4 chord?

A: In the right hand, fingers 1, 3 and 5 or 1, 4 and 5 both work. Choose what lets you move easily to the next chord.
